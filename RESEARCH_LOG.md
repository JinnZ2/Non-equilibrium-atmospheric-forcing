# Research Log

The working record of this project: what was claimed, what happened when it
was run, what survived, and what got revised.

**Why this file exists.** A claim that gets quietly edited leaves no trace, and
the next person cannot tell whether a number is load-bearing or a leftover.
Precedence still carries — the original claim, with its date, is the record of
when the idea was put down. So nothing here is deleted. Superseded artefacts go
to `legacy/` verbatim; the claim they made stays written down below, marked with
what replaced it and why.

**The cycle this log tracks:**

```
hypothesise -> run -> result -> falsified? -> edit the claim
                 ^                                  |
                 |                                  v
                 +---------- rerun <--- search for unknowns
```

**How to rerun everything here:** `python reproduce.py`
(needs NumPy; `pip install numpy matplotlib`)

**Status vocabulary**

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `OPEN` | Stated, not yet tested. |
| `SUPPORTED` | Reproduces from the repo's own model/code. |
| `FALSIFIED` | Ran it; the result contradicts the claim. Claim edited below. |
| `REVISED` | A falsified claim restated so it matches what the run produced. |
| `UNTESTED` | Cannot be settled with what is in this repo. Needs outside data. |

**Scope note.** Everything settled below is *internal consistency* — does the
repo reproduce its own numbers. None of these findings test the core physical
hypothesis (that Al₂O₃ loading raises stratospheric conductivity and amplifies
space-weather coupling). That hypothesis remains `UNTESTED` here; it needs
measurement, not arithmetic. See H-00.

## H-00 — Core hypothesis

> Al₂O₃ nanoparticles from LEO satellite reentry accumulate in the
> stratosphere, raise atmospheric conductivity, and during solar storms
> amplify electromagnetic coupling enough to drive nonlinear economic damage.

- **First stated:** 2025-12-16, `README.md`
- **Status:** `UNTESTED`
- **Why:** Nothing in this repository measures conductivity, field
  amplification, or damages. The models here *assume* the coupling law and
  propagate it; they cannot confirm it. Testing it requires stratospheric
  aerosol measurement and space-weather event data — see U-1, U-2, U-4.
- **What the entries below do and don't say:** they establish that the
  published *numbers* did not follow from the published *model*. That is a
  bookkeeping failure, not a refutation of H-00.

## H-01 — The published projection reproduces from the model

> `coupling_config.json` published: 2025 → 150.2 MT (χ 0.02); 2030 → 480.5
> (χ 0.23); 2035 → 1250.8 (χ 2.10, "Critical"); 2040 → 3100.4 (χ 4.85,
> "Cascade").

- **First stated:** 2025-12-16, `coupling_config.json`
- **Run:** `python reproduce.py`
- **Status:** **`FALSIFIED`**

The repo's own model (`Accumulation-with-coupling.py`, unmodified) produces:

| Year | Published MT | Model MT | Ratio | Published χ | Model χ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 150.2 | 15.0 | 10.0× | 0.02 | 0.00 |
| 2030 | 480.5 | 141.6 | 3.4× | 0.23 | 0.02 |
| 2035 | 1250.8 | 431.3 | 2.9× | 2.10 | 0.22 |
| 2040 | 3100.4 | 1094.1 | 2.8× | 4.85 | 1.97 |

The published series is not noise around the model — it is the model's own
output **shifted five years earlier**:

| Published | Model, +5 years |
|---|---|
| 2025: 150.2 MT, χ 0.02 | 2030: 141.6 MT, χ 0.02 |
| 2030: 480.5 MT, χ 0.23 | 2035: 431.3 MT, χ 0.22 |
| 2035: 1250.8 MT, χ 2.10 | 2040: 1094.1 MT, χ 1.97 |

Three of four points land on the model's value for the year five later, χ
included. The 2040 point drifts (published 3100.4 vs model-2045 2610.3), so
the offset is not perfectly clean, but the pattern is unambiguous.

- **Consequence:** every headline date in the repo ran five years early. This
  is the upstream cause of H-06.
- **Revision:** `projected_time_series` is now generated by
  `reproduce.py --write` and carries a `provenance` block naming the model
  and its parameters. It is no longer hand-authored. The original series is
  preserved at `legacy/coupling_config.2025-12-16.json`.
- **Unknown surfaced:** the offset's origin is not recoverable from git
  history — one commit, no intermediate state. It may have been a start-year
  mismatch (2020 vs 2025 baseline) or a hand-transcribed table. Unresolved.

## H-02 — The repo states one current Al₂O₃ injection rate

> Implicit: the "annual aluminium injection" figure is consistent across the
> README, the config, and the simulations.

- **Run:** `python reproduce.py` (section H-02)
- **Status:** **`FALSIFIED`** — six figures spanning **30×**:

| MT/yr | Source | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 15.0 | `Accumulation-with-coupling.py` | 500 reentries × 30 kg |
| 21.9 | that same rate at the README's reentry count | 730 × 30 kg |
| 40.0 | `README.md` numbers table, 2024 row | 730 reentries → stated ~40 |
| 60.2 | `Satellite-pollution-model.js` | 730 × 550 kg × 15% Al |
| 450.0 | `README.md` executive summary | stated as the **current** rate |
| 460.0 | `README.md` numbers table | 8,400 reentries, Starlink full deploy |

- **Diagnosis:** the executive summary's 450 MT/yr is the *Starlink-full-deploy*
  figure (460) from the README's own table three sections later, presented as
  a present-day rate. The remaining spread is H-03.
- **Revision:** the README executive summary now states the current rate
  consistent with the model and labels the 450–460 figure as the projected
  full-deployment rate it actually is.
- **Still inconsistent:** 15 vs 21.9 vs 40 vs 60.2 are *not* reconciled — they
  depend on the reentry baseline (U-8) and the per-satellite yield (U-1).
  Flagged, not papered over.

**Update 2026-08-14 (via H-10).** External sourcing now settles most of this.
Ferreira et al. (2024) put the 2022 all-reentry Al₂O₃ total at **~17 MT/yr**,
and the mega-constellation scenario at **>360 MT/yr**. Against that:

| Figure | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 15.0 MT/yr (Python model) | **closest to the literature** — within 12% of the 2022 estimate |
| 21.9 / 40 / 60.2 MT/yr | too high for a current rate; all rest on unsourced inputs |
| 450 MT/yr as a *current* rate (README exec summary) | **wrong by ~26×** — and it is not even the right projected figure |
| 460 MT/yr at full deployment (README table) | right order of magnitude vs >360 MT/yr |

So the model was the trustworthy artefact and the prose was not — the reverse
of what the repo's presentation implied. The README's headline figure overstated
the present-day rate by more than an order of magnitude.

## H-03 — "15% aluminium by mass" and "30 kg per 250 kg satellite" agree

> `CLAUDE.md` / `Satellite-pollution-model.js`: satellite aluminium content
> ≈15% of mass. `Aluminum-loading.py`: 30 kg of Al₂O₃ per 250 kg satellite.

- **Run:** `python reproduce.py` (section H-03)
- **Status:** **`FALSIFIED`** — the two differ by **5.2×**.

Al → Al₂O₃ adds oxygen: 101.96 / (2 × 26.98) = **1.890**.

- 15% of a 550 kg satellite = 82.5 kg Al metal → **155.9 kg Al₂O₃** on full oxidation.
- 30 kg Al₂O₃ from a 250 kg satellite → 15.9 kg Al metal → **6.4%** of dry mass.

- **Diagnosis:** the repo uses "aluminum" for both the metal and the oxide, in
  prose and in variable names. The Python tracks oxide mass; the JS tracks
  metal fraction and then labels the result as oxide.
- **Revision:** not silently reconciled — picking one would bake in an
  unsourced choice. Both figures stand, now explicitly labelled by species,
  with the conversion factor recorded. Resolving it needs U-1.

## H-04 — The modelled regime transition is emergent nonlinear behaviour

> The model shows χ jumping from 1.00 (2039) to 1.97 (2040) — read as a phase
> transition, and cited as evidence for threshold behaviour.

- **Run:** `python reproduce.py` (section H-04)
- **Status:** **`FALSIFIED`** — it is an artefact of the coupling function.

`calculate_coupling_coefficient` is piecewise and discontinuous at the branch
point (burden = 1,000 MT):

- below: `(b/1000)²` → at 999.9 MT, χ = **1.1998**
- at/above: `1 + log₁₀(b/1000 + 1) × 2` → at 1000.1 MT, χ = **1.9225**

The two branches do not meet. χ jumps **1.60×** across a 0.2 MT change in
burden. The 2039→2040 "transition" is that step, sampled annually — it would
appear at whatever year the burden crosses 1,000 MT, under any growth rate,
with no physics involved.

- **Revision:** the discontinuity is **left in the code deliberately** —
  `CONTRIBUTING.md` says do not smooth or normalise to hide nonlinearities,
  and silently patching it would erase the finding. It is now documented in
  the function's docstring as a known artefact, so it cannot be mistaken for
  a result. Replacing the law is real work, not a smoothing pass — see U-2.
- **Note:** this does not show threshold behaviour is absent. It shows *this
  model does not evidence it*. The two are different claims.

## H-05 — Cascade-failure threshold is χ > 5.0

> `coupling_config.json` (2025-12-16): `cascade_failure: { chi_limit: 5.0 }`.
> `CLAUDE.md`: "Cascade Failure (>5.0)".

- **Status:** **`REVISED`** → cascade begins at **χ ≥ 3.0**.
- **Conflict:** `Accumulation-with-coupling.py` has always labelled
  `CASCADE FAILURE` at χ ≥ 3.0. The documented regimes ran Nominal <0.5,
  Incipient 0.5–1.5, Systemic 1.5–3.0, Cascade >5.0 — leaving **3.0–5.0
  unassigned**, which is why the code and the config disagreed.
- **Resolution:** each regime's `chi_max` is the next regime's floor, and
  cascade starts where systemic fragility ends, at 3.0. This matches the code
  that actually produced every published number. `risk_thresholds` now uses
  explicit `chi_min`/`chi_max`.
- **Unresolved:** what the original 5.0 referred to is not recoverable. It may
  have been a distinct "hard cascade" marker rather than the regime floor. If
  so it should come back as a fifth named regime, not as a redefinition of
  this one.
- **Effect on dates:** χ ≥ 3.0 is first reached in **2049** (5,139 MT). Under
  the old 5.0 reading it would be 2061 (37,793 MT).

## H-06 — "Year of Collapse" is 2035–2038

> `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY_STRATEGIC_RISK.md`: "The 'Year of Collapse' is currently
> modeled at 2035–2038, assuming current satellite launch trajectories."
> `CLAUDE.md`: "critical cascade threshold projected for 2035-2038 at ~1,000
> MT cumulative burden."

- **Run:** `python reproduce.py` (threshold crossings)
- **Status:** **`FALSIFIED`** as stated — **`REVISED`** below.

The repo's own model, at its own parameters:

| Crossing | Year | Burden | χ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incipient (χ > 0.5) | 2038 | 762 MT | 0.70 |
| Systemic fragility (χ > 1.5) | 2040 | 1,094 MT | 1.97 |
| Cascade failure (χ ≥ 3.0) | 2049 | 5,139 MT | 3.09 |

The ~1,000 MT burden is reached in **2040**, not 2035–2038. Nothing the repo
labels "cascade" occurs before 2049.

- **Traced to:** H-01. Against the five-years-early published series, 2035 did
  read as χ 2.10 "Critical" — so 2035–2038 was a faithful reading of a series
  that itself did not reproduce. One bookkeeping error, propagated into the
  policy brief's headline.
- **Revision:** stated as *2040 for the ~1,000 MT / systemic-fragility
  crossing, 2049 for cascade, under this model's assumptions*, with the
  assumptions named. The README and executive summary now carry the revised
  dates and point here.
- **Caveat that cuts the other way:** these dates are only as good as the
  18%/yr growth rate and the 500/yr baseline, neither of which is sourced
  (U-8). A higher baseline pulls every date earlier, and the repo contains a
  higher one: rerunning at the README's own **730 reentries/yr** moves the
  1,000 MT crossing from 2040 to **2038** — inside the originally claimed
  window. So the 2035–2038 headline is reachable from the repo's own
  alternative baseline; it just was not reachable from the series that was
  actually published, which is what H-01 falsified. The date was right for
  the wrong reason, and which baseline is correct is still open (U-8).

## H-08 — `Aluminum-loading.py` is a distinct model

- **Status:** **`REVISED`** — superseded, moved to `legacy/`.
- **Finding:** its accumulation core is reproduced inside
  `Accumulation-with-coupling.py` — same 30 kg/satellite, same 30-year
  `np.roll` residence buffer, same exponential launch growth — which adds the
  χ term. It is the earlier draft, not a parallel approach.
- **Kept because:** it is the first written statement of the accumulation
  model and carries the earlier date. Precedence.
- **Bug found on the way out, and it is inherited:** the comment reads
  *"Using a simple linear decay model for atmospheric fallout"*, but the
  implementation is a **rectangular** kernel — 100% retention for exactly 30
  years, then total instantaneous removal. There is no decay of any kind. The
  successor inherits this. See U-3.

## H-09 — Al₂O₃ is the species that matters

> Implicit throughout the repo: aluminium oxide is *the* reentry pollutant.
> Every model, parameter and headline number is Al₂O₃-only.

- **Run:** literature search, 2026-08-14; `python Multi-species-accumulation.py`
- **Status:** **`FALSIFIED`** as an exclusive claim.

Murphy et al. (2023, PNAS) report **over 20 elements** from spacecraft reentry
in stratospheric aerosol, in ratios matching spacecraft alloys. Specifically:

- **~10%** of stratospheric sulfuric acid particles larger than 120 nm already
  contain aluminium and other spacecraft-reentry elements.
- The reentry mass of **lithium, aluminium, copper and lead each exceeds the
  cosmic dust influx** of that same metal. These are not trace contaminants;
  for these four elements the space industry has overtaken the natural source.
- **Niobium and hafnium** are the attribution keys: neither occurs as a free
  element in nature, so their presence is unambiguous spacecraft origin with
  no meteoric confound.
- Projection: planned constellation growth could raise the affected fraction
  to roughly **50%**, comparable to the fraction now containing meteoric metals.

- **Revision:** the species inventory is now explicit in `species_inventory.json`
  (13 entries), and `Multi-species-accumulation.py` reports across all of them.
  Al₂O₃ remains the only species with a flux estimate — **1 of 13, or 8%
  coverage**. The model states that gap in its output rather than hiding it.
- **What this does *not* say:** that the other species couple electromagnetically.
  Detection is not coupling. See U-12 — the weights that would let them enter
  the χ law do not exist, and the model refuses to invent them.

## H-10 — The 30 kg/250 kg satellite yield is unsourced (U-1)

- **Status:** **`RESOLVED`** — U-1 closed, and the repo's figure holds up.

The number traces to **Ferreira et al. (2024), GRL** `10.1029/2024GL109280`: a
typical 250 kg satellite generates ~30 kg of Al₂O₃ nanoparticles on demise.
It was never invented here — it was just never cited.

Two independent checks now pass:

| Quantity | Repo model | Literature | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Al₂O₃ per 250 kg satellite | 30 kg | 30 kg | exact |
| Implied current annual flux | 15.0 MT/yr (500 × 30 kg) | ~17 MT/yr (2022, all reentries) | within 12% |
| Residence time | 30 yr | up to 30 yr, mesosphere → ozone layer | consistent |

- **Consequence for H-02:** the spread of injection figures now resolves in
  favour of the *Python model*, not the README. See the H-02 revision below.
- **Caveat on residence time:** the literature figure is a *settling time* —
  particles descending through the stratosphere, destroying ozone the whole
  way. This repo implements it as a rectangular kernel: fully inert for 30
  years, then vanishing. Same number, different physics. U-3 stands and is
  now better understood: the error is not the duration, it is treating the
  transit as inert.

## H-11 — Satellite reentry is the pathway

> The repo models reentry ablation only. Launch is not mentioned as an
> emission source anywhere.

- **Status:** **`FALSIFIED`** — there is a second pathway, and it deposits the
  same species.

Solid rocket motors burn aluminium fuel with an ammonium perchlorate oxidiser.
They emit **alumina — chemically the same Al₂O₃ the reentry model tracks** —
plus hydrogen chloride. Published launch emission inventories cover chlorine,
NOx, H₂O, CO₂, black carbon and alumina.

- **Consequence:** every total-atmospheric-Al₂O₃ figure this repo publishes is
  an **undercount**, by whatever the launch pathway contributes. That margin is
  unquantified here.
- **Revision:** `alumina_launch` is now a distinct inventory entry cross-linked
  to `al2o3_reentry` via `same_species_as`, so the double pathway is visible in
  the data rather than buried in prose. The launch pathway is 4 of 13 species
  and **0 of them are quantified**.
- **Not claimed:** that the two pathways are interchangeable. They may differ in
  deposition altitude and particle size distribution, both of which would matter
  for coupling. Unknown.

## H-12 — Al₂O₃ destroys ozone directly

> `Coupling-Physics.md` §4 models catalytic ozone destruction as an Al₂O₃
> surface process. `README.md`: "Aluminum particles act as catalysts for ozone
> destruction — direct catalytic reactions."

- **Status:** **`FALSIFIED`** — the mechanism is indirect, and that changes what
  the model should be coupled to.

Per Ferreira et al. (2024): aluminium oxides **do not react with ozone**. They
activate **chlorine**, which does the destroying. The alumina is not consumed,
so one particle keeps catalysing for decades as it settles.

This is the most consequential finding in this round, because of what it implies
for a project whose entire thesis is coupling:

> **The reentry pathway's ozone impact is co-limited by a launch-pathway
> species.** Al₂O₃ supplies reaction surface; chlorine supplies the destruction.
> Doubling alumina against fixed chlorine is not the same as doubling both.
> Satellite reentry and rocket launches are therefore **not two independent
> problems** — and this repo models neither the coupling nor the chlorine.

- **Revision:** recorded in `species_inventory.json` under `hcl_launch`
  (`criticality_note`) and surfaced in every `Multi-species-accumulation.py` run.
  `Coupling-Physics.md` §4 is now flagged as describing the wrong mechanism.
- **Tension surfaced, unresolved:** the retrieved chlorine sensitivity is
  *modest* — a 10× increase in rocket chlorine over 2019 gives <0.1 DU (0.04%)
  near-global column ozone loss; 52× gives 0.6 DU (0.23%). Those are far smaller
  than this repo's ozone rhetoric implies. Either the repo's ozone claims are
  overstated, or they rest on a mechanism the cited work does not cover. This
  should be settled, not left ambiguous. See U-13.
